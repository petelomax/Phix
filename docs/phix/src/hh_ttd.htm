time travel debugging

There is a pretty inspirational video here, however the "live GUI" aspect, as impressive (and actually severely limited) as it is, is not what we're really after. [I can imagine a way to implement such a Gameboy-level live GUI on a much more powerful PC: write a display engine that works entirely off a reasonably small fixed-size block of memory, monitor/keep a history of that block, and embed a second copy of the display engine in the debugger which works entirely off the copy. But I couldn't scale that to full PC graphics capabilities, that is assuming saving untold gigabytes of individual screen images ends up being impractical.]

Phix supports (or rather one day hopefully will) an ever so slightly limited form of "time travel debugging":
  • The debugger maintains a small circular buffer of things it has already shown you which allows it to mimic "step back".
  • Incidentally, the buffer values are all just the string representations, which skirts any refcount/deep_copy() required issues.
  • Said buffer also contains a history of the line numbers it actually touched on, it is not actually analysing anything at all.
  • Once no longer at the execution "tip", you cannot change anything, in particular view variables not already in said buffer.
  • A "restart" option tries very hard to get you back, pretty much instantly, to the same line you are currently on, only now the "tip".
  • Obviously a restart restores the watched variables and copes with sources being edited under your feet, eg line 1724 is now 1727.
  • Note restart is "stop here" when a specified set of variables meet explicitly specified conditions, rather than any "voodoo magic".
  • Should said fail to trigger then I'm afraid your time here is over and you are pretty much forced right back to square one.
  • You may/will often need to trap earlier in the execution, to populate said buffer with the information needed to step back.
  • The latter is often just a restart after editing in a new trace(1), which it will seem to effectively completely ignore.
  • However be warned inadvertently asking it to quietly skip say 250 million instructions is likely to take a while...
  • There are no massive data dumps, severe technical difficulties, or any attempt to defeat non-deterministic behaviour.
  • Naturally, chasing (say) low-level bugs in the heap manager may be made nigh on impossible by the mere presence of any debugger.
  • In other words the latter must continue to rely on x32dbg/x86dbg/edb aka things that don't mess with (eg) the Phix heap.
Step one is to create a "fake debugger", which just displays some random source and imaginary variables with a hard-coded list of stepping points. That is all purely look-and-feel, with no need whatsoever for any clever low-level technical realities. That said, should you want any snazzy fancy tricks to improve the data visualisation and/or navigation, now is the time to add them. There may need to be (for instance) "stop earlier" and "start single stepping when" variants of restart, and again it is going to be much better if such are predicted/planned up front, than later bemoan they weren't properly thought about earlier. Likewise should you be able to recall some tricky bug, and with the benefit of 20:20 hindsight imagine what might have helped, create a mock-up with sufficient detail it manages to convince someone [else] to at least try and implement it for real.

It remains undecided as to whether this will be a new standalone theDebugger.exw or integrated in theEditor.exw, but one thing for sure is that while p.exe may have the necessary hooks, system_exec(), shared memory, and/or lock stepping, the new debugger will not be part of it, unlike the previous one, and it may even end up significantly smaller because of that, not that said is an important goal.
